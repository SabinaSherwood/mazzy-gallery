Studies in Stainless,

14 Archival Plates · 3 Exhibition Wings · 1 Undermount Basin

In this landmark fourteen-part architectural intervention, multidisciplinary artist Mazzy subverts the utilitarian tyranny of the suburban kitchen basin. Embodying The Third Cat—simultaneously fluid and solid, domestic subject and sovereign witness—the artist asks: Is the cat inhabiting the sink, or is the sink merely defining the boundaries of the cat? The Third Cat declines to choose.

// CURATORIAL CATALOGUE ESSAY

On the Domestic Basin: Mazzy & "The Third Cat"

To consider Mazzy within the eighteen-gauge stainless-steel basin is to confront the fundamental paradox of domestic spatial occupation: a vessel engineered for things that pass through it, occupied by a being that declines to leave. Where the canine approaches household architecture with compliance toward utility, the tortoiseshell cat approaches the sink as a site of ontological inquiry.

Across these fourteen plates, arranged in three exhibition wings, Kazimierz Dąbrowski’s positive disintegration is translated into plumbing: the basin’s utilitarian identity is shattered and reintegrated into a higher-order reliquary of contemplation. Agency, not accident, is the medium. Mazzy is not placed in the sink; she chooses it. She is not the cat inside the box but its observer.

Logic builds the house. Lived experience inhabits it. The Third Cat, seated in the basin beneath the window, realizes the house is transparent.
